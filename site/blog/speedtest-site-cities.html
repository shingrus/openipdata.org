title: City from M-Lab speed test servers date: 2026-11-20 summary: A client that reaches an M-Lab speed test server within 5 ms is very likely in the city of that server. This gives a new city layer. ---

M-Lab runs speed test servers in many cities and publishes its measurements as open data (CC0). For each client /24 the data records the lowest round-trip time to each server. A client that answers a server within a few milliseconds cannot be far from it.

The rule

A /24 gets the city of the nearest server when its lowest reply time is at most 5 ms. The city must be a GeoNames place in the server's country; one site of 80 is not a GeoNames place and gets no row. The layer is 35-speedtest-site, and it only adds cities: it never changes a country.

Why 5 ms

We tried limits from 2 to 10 ms on RIPE Atlas devices:

LimitRightLost
3 ms+46-7
5 ms+63-13
8 ms+72-20
9 ms+77-20
10 ms+80-28

Above 5 ms, an added answer is right only 27% to 40% of the time, so we stopped there.

Accuracy

A rebuild from an export of 21 days (3.05M rows, 1.66M client /24s) gave 401,813 rows. Right devices rose by 63 and 13 were lost: right city went from 59.14% to 59.49%. The layer's own city was right for 64% of the 372 devices it answers.

This is below the M-Lab hop city (36-mlab), so it sits under it in the order of layers. The export is made by hand about once a month.

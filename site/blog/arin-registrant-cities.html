title: City from small ARIN records, by RDAP date: 2026-11-19 summary: ARIN publishes no whois dump, so we ask its RDAP service about one address per /24 and use small records, schools and local governments. ---

RIPE, APNIC, AFRINIC and LACNIC publish their whois data. ARIN does not. To use the registrant city of ARIN records we ask its RDAP service instead, and the result is the layer 37-whois-arin.

The walk

The walk covers the IPv4 /24s where our own published file has a country but only a weak city or none. For each one we ask about a single address, at two requests a second, and keep the range, country, state and city of the registrant. A /24 is asked again after 180 days.

Which records are used

A record gives a city only when GeoNames places the city in the record's own state. Records of at most a /20 are used. Records up to a /16 are used too when the registrant is a school or a local government. Only this class is kept, never a name.

The reason is how the city of a record behaves. A record wider than a /16 usually holds the head office of a company, and its city was right for only 9% of the devices. For schools the city was right for 78% (29 of 37 devices), for local governments 11 of 11, and for everyone else 26%.

Accuracy

On RIPE Atlas devices, the layer's city was right for 61% of 531 rows (records of at most a /20). It added 197 right devices and lost 37.

The wider records are not wasted. For address space that no network announces, the city of the holder block is used as a weak guess, 85-holder-city, below every real layer.

No visitor address and no registrant name is stored by this layer.
